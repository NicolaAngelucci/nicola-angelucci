Biography

Nicola Angelucci, born in 1979 and originally from Altino (Chieti), is a drummer and composer.

He has received numerous awards and scholarships, including a scholarship at the prestigious Columbia College in Chicago in 2000. He won the “Baronissi Jazz” competition in 2000 and 2004, the “Gran Prix du Public” at the “Tramplin Jazz d’Avignon” in 2002, and the “26th Hoeilaart Jazz Contest” in Belgium in 2004. He taught at the “Orsara Jazz” workshops (Foggia) in 2005 and 2006 and at the Eddie Lang Jazz Festival in 2012. His discography includes approximately 70 recordings as a sideman, four albums as a leader, and four as a co-leader.

His first album under his own name, recorded mostly in New York and released in 2010 by Via Veneto Jazz, is “The First One”, featuring Sam Yahel and Roberto Tarenzi on organ and piano, Jeremy Pelt on trumpet, Paolo Recchia on saxophone, and Kengo Nakamura, Johannes Weidenmueller and Francesco Puglisi on bass.

The second, “Beyond the Drums” (2013), was recorded again with Recchia, Tarenzi and Puglisi, with whom Nicola had formed a close-knit quartet. The line-up also features a special guest: New York guitarist Peter Bernstein.

In 2021 he released his third album as a leader, “Changes” (Warner Music / Flying Sparks), made up entirely of his original compositions. It was recorded with Olivia Trummer on piano and vocals, who also wrote the lyrics, Luca Bulgareli on double bass, and Gabriele Mirabassi on clarinet.

In 2023, as co-leader with pianist and vocalist Olivia Trummer, he released “Dialogue’s Delight” (Warner Music / Flying Sparks), a duo album featuring Luciano Biondini on accordion.

In 2026 he released his fourth album as a leader, “Identithree” (GleAM Records), with Enrico Zanisi on piano and Enzo Pietropaoli on double bass.

Since 2013 he has been a member of Fabrizio Bosso’s quartet and has worked regularly with Bebo Ferra, Rosario Bonaccorso, Olivia Trummer, Dado Moroni, Max Ionata and Paolo Recchia.

He has been a sideman for many of the most prominent and celebrated leaders in jazz, including Benny Golson (with whom he worked for seven years in Italy and abroad, from 2004 to 2011), Wynton Marsalis, Dee Dee Bridgewater, Mike Stern, Bob James, Steve Grossman, Sonny Fortune, Wess Anderson, Jeremy Pelt, Joel Frahm, Eddie Gomez, Andy Gravish, Jim Rotondi, Alex Sipiagin, Sam Yahel, Jerry Bergonzi, Aaron Goldberg, Larry Granadier, Peter Bernstein, Dave Kikoski, John Hicks, George Garzone and Joey De Francesco.

His most notable recordings and collaborations also include work with Enrico Pieranunzi, Paolo Fresu, Enrico Rava, Max Ionata, Francesco Cafiso, Rosario Giuliani, Mario Biondi, Bob Mintzer, Alex Sipiagin, Peter Bernstein, Sam Yahel, Jeremy Pelt, Mike Stern, Rita Marcotulli, Fabrizio Bosso, Dado Moroni, Andrea Pozza, Perico Sambeat, Gabriele Mirabassi, Gegè Telesforo, Luca Mannutza, Nico Gori, Maurizio Rolli, Maurizio Giammarco, Stefania Tallini, Susanna Stivali and Javier Girotto.
